# Grease Fire: sources

Every rule in this film comes from published public guidance, and it is US guidance. Nothing here is added to it. Checked 3 November 2026. Elsewhere the advice differs: the London Fire Brigade says not to tackle a pan fire yourself at all (https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/the-home/cooking/pan-fires), so check the local fire service before publishing this film outside the US. It also backs the "fireball" that water makes.

| What the film says | Source |
|---|---|
| Rule 1: no water | Indiana Department of Homeland Security, Cooking Safety (citing the NFPA): "Never try using water to douse a grease fire." https://www.in.gov/dhs/get-prepared/files/cooking-safety.pdf |
| It sinks under the hot oil, flashes to steam, and throws the fire at you | Mental Floss, on the chemistry: water sinks below the oil, turns to steam and pushes burning oil outward. https://mentalfloss.com/article/64498/why-you-shouldnt-use-water-extinguish-grease-fire Oliver Fire Department, on the result: an explosion-type effect. https://www.eaglevalleynews.com/news/video-dont-throw-water-on-stove-top-grease-fire-oliver-fire-department/ This is the mechanism, not the instruction, and it is the weakest source in the film. |
| Rule 2: if it is small, slide a lid over the pan, and turn off the burner | Indiana DHS, same document: "Smother small grease fires by sliding a lid over the pan and turning off the stovetop." US Fire Administration: "Keep a pan lid or baking sheet nearby. Use it to cover the pan if it catches on fire." https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/ |
| Do not carry it to the sink | Indiana DHS, same document: "Leave the lid on and do not try to carry the pan away." The sink is the film's example. |
| Rule 3: the lid stays on until the pan is cool | NFPA tips, in the Nevada State Fire Marshal's handout: leave the pan covered until it is completely cooled. https://fire.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/firenvgov/content/Home/Features/CookingSafety2020.pdf Read as a search excerpt. |
| Rule 4: any doubt? Get out, shut the door, then call | Indiana DHS, same document: "Evacuate the home and call 911 if the cooking fire becomes too large to address safely." US Fire Administration, Home Fire Escape Plans: "Close the door behind you." https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/home-fire-escape-plans/ NFPA: "When in doubt, just get out." https://www.fire.nv.gov/siteassets/home/features/CookingSafety2020.pdf On screen the film says "call for help", since 911 is only some countries' number. |
| Tonight: put a lid next to the stove | US Fire Administration, as above: "Keep a pan lid or baking sheet nearby." |

## Not claims

- The flame's face, the oven mitt and the kitchen are a period cartoon. The fire on screen is drawn to be read, not to scale.
- The film covers a pan fire on a stovetop only.
