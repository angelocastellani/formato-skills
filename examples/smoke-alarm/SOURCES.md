# Smoke Alarm: sources

Every step in this film comes from published public guidance. Nothing here is added to it. Checked 3 November 2026.

| What the film says | Source |
|---|---|
| A fire can turn deadly in two minutes | Ready.gov, Home Fires: "A fire can become life-threatening in just two minutes." https://www.ready.gov/home-fires |
| Step 1: leave your things, not your wallet, not your laptop | American Red Cross, What To Do If A Fire Starts: leave your things where they are and save yourself. https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/if-a-fire-starts.html Read as a search excerpt; the page refuses automated reading. The phone and the wallet are the film's examples of "things". |
| Step 2: if there is smoke, do not stand up; crawl under it | Ready.gov, same page: "Drop down to the floor and crawl low, under any smoke to your exit. Heavy smoke and poisonous gases collect first along the ceiling." |
| Step 3: feel the knob and the door first | Ready.gov, same page: "Before opening a door, feel the doorknob and door." |
| If it is hot, or smoke comes around it, leave it shut and use your second way out | Ready.gov, same page: "If either is hot, or if there is smoke coming around the door, leave the door closed and use your second way out." |
| Step 4: close the doors behind you; it slows the fire | American Red Cross, What To Do If A Fire Starts: close doors behind you as you escape. https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/if-a-fire-starts.html (The USFA's "Close the door behind you" is written for high-rises.) UL's Fire Safety Research Institute on why a closed door slows a fire: https://fsri.org/program-update/close-you-doze-series-after |
| Step 5: call from outside, and never go back in | American Red Cross, Fire Prevention Week: get out and stay out, never go back inside for people, pets or things; get out safely, then dial 911. https://www.redcross.org/about-us/news-and-events/news/2024/fire-prevention-week-protect-your-household-by-practicing-home-fire-escape-plan-testing-smoke-alarms.html Read as a search excerpt. |
| In one test burn, an open bedroom reached 500 degrees, the one with the door shut 100 | UL FSRI, Close Before You Doze: peak temperatures 4 ft above the floor, 500°F in the open bedroom and 100°F in the closed bedroom. https://fsri.org/program-update/close-you-doze-series-after The film says "one test burn" because the figures differ between tests, and press often rounds this one to 1,000°F. |
| Tonight: close your bedroom door | UL FSRI, same page: the campaign's advice is to close your bedroom door at night. |

## Not claims

- The escape countdown on screen starts at the Ready.gov two minutes and runs in real time. It is a picture of urgency, not a promise of how long anyone has.
- The figure, the room and the window are illustrations. A window is one example of a second way out.
