# Rip Current: sources

Every step in this film comes from published public guidance. Nothing here is added to it. Checked 3 November 2026.

| What the film says | Source |
|---|---|
| "That is probably a rip current" | The beach moving away is a strong sign and not a diagnosis, hence "probably". NWS: https://www.weather.gov/news/232606-ripcurrent-safety |
| A rip can move faster than an Olympic swimmer | NOAA National Ocean Service: "Moving at speeds of up to eight feet per second, rip currents can move faster than an Olympic swimmer." https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/ripcurrent.html |
| Quest 1: stop swimming toward the beach; straight back drains your stamina | NOAA, same page: "don't fight it", and swimming straight to shore can lead to exhaustion. National Weather Service: "Don't swim against the current." https://www.weather.gov/news/232606-ripcurrent-safety |
| Quest 2: breathe and float | National Weather Service, same news page: stay calm, "Relax and float first." |
| It pulls you away from shore, not under | National Weather Service, same news page: rip currents pull you away from shore, not under the water. |
| Quest 3: if you can swim, try sideways along the beach, then angle back in | National Weather Service, same news page: move parallel to the shoreline, then angle back toward the beach. NOAA: swim parallel to the shore, then angle back toward land. |
| Rips are often narrow | NWS, rip current FAQs: most are narrow, though width varies. https://www.weather.gov/media/safety/ripcurrent_faqs.pdf NOAA: "powerful, narrow channels of fast-moving water." |
| Quest 4: too tired? Float until it weakens, then angle in | National Park Service, Cape Hatteras: "If you cannot swim out of the current, float until it weakens, then swim diagonally toward the shore." https://home.nps.gov/caha/learn/news/rip-currents-can-be-deadly.htm (archived page, last updated 2015). National Weather Service, Morehead City: "If you are unable to swim out of the rip current, float or calmly tread water." https://www.weather.gov/mhx/RipCurrentsInfo |
| Quest 5: any time you can't make it, face the beach, wave your arms, and yell | National Weather Service news page: if you think you cannot reach shore, face the beach, wave your arms and yell for help. |
| Rips often form on calm, sunny days | National Weather Service: "Rip currents often form on calm, sunny days." https://www.weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent |
| Tonight: look up the surf forecast | National Weather Service news page: check your local surf zone and beach hazards forecasts before you leave. |

## Not claims

- "Treadmill, not drain" is the film's own comparison for the same fact, away from shore and not under.
- The BEACH distance, the stamina bar and the race bars are a game's picture of the situation, not measurements. The only real speed claim is the one NOAA makes.
- The picture is a toy. This is not a swimming lesson, and the film does not tell anyone that they are safe.
