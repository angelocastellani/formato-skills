# Pass the Note: sources

A what-if is impossible on purpose; what is said about it is not. Every number on screen is `0.1 mm × 2^fold`, worked in `scenes.tsx`.

| Claim on screen | Number used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A sheet of ordinary paper is about 0.1 mm thick, and each fold doubles the thickness | 0.1 mm, ×2 | [Scientific American: How a sheet of paper can take you to the moon](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-many-paper-folds-would-get-you-to-the-moon/) |
| Fold 42 passes the Moon; half of that was the last fold | 439,805 km against an average 384,400 km; fold 41 is 219,902 km | Same article; distance from [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/moon-distance/en/) |

## Rough on purpose, and said so

- "about a thumb" (12.8 mm), "a loaf of bread" (20.5 cm), "a grown-up" (1.64 m), "two floors" (6.55 m), "four floors" (13.1 m),
  "about eight floors" (26.2 m): household comparisons at about 3.3 m a floor, not measurements.
- The stepped spire in the skyline shot is 828 m tall and stands for no building in particular.

## The cheat in the picture

The film keeps the note wide enough to see. Each fold also halves its width, so at fold 42 a real note would be about a tenth of a
micron across, smaller than a bacterium, per the Scientific American article. The film never claims otherwise; if you want it said,
this is the line to add.

Nobody in this film is real. Leo, Ivy, Ben and Mrs Park are invented.
